Summary[]

At the Margherita's Restaurant, you meet Carlotta. While sharing a meal, you overhear a rumor about the "Golden Anthem." It's said that playing their last works can summon the Daemons. Suddenly, the melody begins to play, and the so-called Daemons quietly appears behind you. Cristoforo approaches and greets you, mentioning a recent article from Ragunna that offers an unusual take on the "Golden Anthem" legend.

Carlotta then leads you to Ciaccona, who seems hesitant to involve you in the mystery. After some persuasion, she agrees to help. To uncover the truth, Ciaccona decides to play the melody once more. The Daemons reappear. After defeating them, Ciaccona draws closer, hoping to use her Forte to uncover the story hidden in the frequencies. To learn more, you need more melodies. Ciaccona takes you to a moonlit shore, a place where Ragunna's poets and musicians are known to bury their instruments. There, you might find new clues.

You discover the instruments of the Poets Society, and Ciaccona decides to use it to recreate the past. In the Realm of Melodies, you witness the Poets Society facing interrogation by the Order. Despite the pressure, Faroll, a member of the Society, does not resist, but calmly accepts whatever comes his way. As you touch Faroll's Remnant melody, it becomes clear tha the had already foreseen this fate. The truth behind their capture was not blasphemy, but the discovery of the true face of the Sentinel. You encounter Rimbaud's Remnant melody. Unlike Faroll, he believes the truth should remain buried forever, never to be shared through poems. You then reach Selena's Remnant melody. She understands Faroll's choice, seeing that while they could have let the truth buried with them, they still had the choice to believe and hope. It was this hope that led Faroll to share the truth through his poems. As you wander through the Realm of Melodies, you lose track of Ciaccona and find yourself in a study, where a young girl is singing. As you follow her, she mentions Golden Anthem poems as well as the name Ciaccona Toccata.

Finally out of the Realm of Melodies, you decide to head for th Cathedral of Mercury, thinking you might find more clues. Along the way, you meet Raisa, who is becoming increasingly concerned about the Poets Society's past works. You and Ciaccona try to help her regain her confidence. Finding a new motivation to sing, Raisa and her choir allows you to listen to their rehersal. Inside the Realm of Melodies, you discover the Poets Society was criticized for glorifying the knights over the common folk during one of their hymn performances. Their assumptions were challenged, so they traveled to the knight's encampment at Whisperwind Haven to verify the truth for themselves.

After returning from the Realm of Melodies, you share what you've learned with Ciaccona. You realize that the key to understanding the Poets Society's change of attitude might lie in their time in the Cathedral, a turning point that led them to seek the truth and resist the Order's deception.

You find something that carries the melody&#8212;the wind. The wind is everlasting, recording and bringing back the Poets Society's melodies. Adopting the Poet Society's mindset, Ciaccona plays the melody once more, and the Society members' voices ring again. The members hid a lithograph here. They were actually guided by a mysterious figure, but there's no time to investigate who they are now. What's most important is returning to the restaurant to change the public's view of the "Golden Anthem."

As you finish your interpretation, the crowd in the restaurant grows restless, many surrounding you with questions. While you and Ciaccona are separated in the crowd, Cristoforo appears next to Ciaccona, telling a story about a caged bird, as if hinting at something deeper. Carlotta greets you and shares that, through tracking the article's origin, she has uncovered some new leads. Just as you are about to share everything with Ciaccona, she disappears without a word. Carlotta sighs that Ciaccona has never changed since childhood, always vanishing without a word.

Cristoforo's words echo in Ciaccona's mind. As she walks toward the harbor, memories of the Poets Society and her own past resurface. She now understands what Cristoforo meant&#8212;if the truth is revealed, the past tragedies may be doomed to repeat themselves.

After touching the sheet music Ciaccona left behind, you're transported back into the Realm of Melodies, where you meet the girl from the study once more. This time, she introduces herself properly as Ciaccona.

Ciaccona joins you in returning to Ragunna City, where, as planned, you will make your final revelation. However, your actions have drawn the attention of the order, and the Acolytes are closing in on you. After escaping the city, Ciaccona is still filled with excitement for finally revealing the truth. You stop for a moment, and in her emotional state, she plays a song for you. Once the performance is over, Ciaccona warns you to watch out for a playwright in the city. Being wanted as a criminal, she will soon need to run again. This is your farewell, though not the last time you will meet.

At the Cathedral of Mercury, Cristoforo reveals Fenrico's plan. In the confrontation, Fenrico suspect that Cristoforo was the one who secretly guided the Poets Society all those years ago.

"Cultural Insights"Literary works draw their vitality from two sources: The creator and the readers.

The creator gives it new life, while the readers help extend it through reading. Each time they share a piece of work or publish their thoughts and reviews, they breathe new life into it.

Some may find the term "life of a work" elusive, as it's too abstract and intangible.

However, a parallel can be easily drawn from human life. When discussing the concept of life, one essential topic is inevitably brought up&#8212;name. A name is a symbol of existence, one of the most important markers that separate individual lives. If one desires to feel the vitality of a work, the most direct approach would be to feel its name, the various names bestowed by creators and readers.

I once heard an eerie tale from a land in the east that spoke of a lady with skin as fair as snow who would stand in a snowstorm, cradling a wailing baby in her arms, begging the passersby to hold the baby for her. If some kindhearted individual agrees to that request, they'd soon find themselves unable to let go, bound to freeze to death.

Intriguing, isn't it? But, by this point, it's a fascinating tale that evokes many distinct images that cannot yet form the entirety of "life." 

But what if we give it a name? Like... "The Snow Woman." This name alone captures the entire narrative and defines its essence.

However, even with the name anchoring people's imaginations, the unusual nature of words means each person takes a narrative differently.

Hence... The life of a creative work is less controllable than human life.

So, if one day, people's understanding of a work is led astray, then words could turn into a devastating weapon.

What would we do then? Show anger? No, absolutely not! Anger is the cheapest outlet of dissent. As creators, all we can do is believe. Believe that our words can live in their most genuine form.

Glossary[]

- Celestial Steed - The autumn night's brightest constellation, shrouded in legends. A poet of Golden Anthem once wrote, "I am a night hawk of wings aflame, soaring towards the Celestial Steed, whose praises I sing and whose true form I behold." Such imagery always evokes Rinascita's Sentinel. But why soar towards it and behold its true form? Maybe the poem was intended to convey some message.

- Golden Anthem - Once a reputable poets society in Ragunna, consisting of three members: Faroll, Selena, and Rimbaud. They composed numerous anthems and poems glorifying the Sentinel and the Order, but were later accused of blasphemy by the Order and sent on a Pilgrim's Sail. They vanished thereafter without a trace, and mentioning of their names is now forbidden in Ragunna.

- Melody - Music is composed of frequency, as is everything. Thus, everything hums its own melody. Ciaccona's Forte allows her to perceive these melodies; by returning them, she crafts a Sonoro Sphere in her hands where stories and memeories carried in music replay themselves. The deeper her understanding of the melodies, the richer the stories she can summon.

- Requiem Paradise - A shore where Rinascitan poets and musiccians bury their retired instruments.

- The Golden Bough - An ancient epic poem of Rinascita. It pioneered blending various regional folkfore with the tales of the Sentinel, including the first accounts of the Carnevale's origins.
